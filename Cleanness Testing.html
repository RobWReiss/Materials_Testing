Inclusion Rating Simulator

Look inside a polished piece of steel and judge how clean it is (ASTM E45, Method A, simplified).

New to this? What the words mean
Non-metallic inclusions
Tiny particles of non-steel material, such as oxides and sulfides, that get trapped in steel when it is made.
Why they matter
A crack can start at an inclusion. Cleaner steel lasts longer, which matters most in parts that are loaded over and over, such as bearings, shafts and aircraft landing gear.
How it is checked
A slice of steel is polished flat and viewed under a microscope at 100 times magnification. What is seen is compared with a set of reference pictures, called chart plates.
The worst field
A "field" is one microscope view. The field with the most inclusions is the worst field, and that is the one that gets rated.
Severity
A score from 0.5 (very few) up to 3 (a lot), in half steps. Pick the chart plate that looks most like your field.
Thin or heavy
Thin inclusions are fine and narrow. Heavy ones are wide and chunky. Heavy ones do more harm, so the limits are stricter for them.
