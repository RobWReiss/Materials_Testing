New to this? What the words mean
Stress and strain
Stress is the pulling force divided by the bar's cross-section area (MPa). Strain is how much longer it gets, as a percentage of its starting length.
Young's modulus (E)
Stiffness. The slope of the straight part of the curve. Steel is about 200 GPa whatever its strength.
Yield strength
Where the steel stops springing back and starts to stretch permanently. Some mild steels show a sudden peak and dip, others bend gradually, so we use one method for every steel: the 0.2% proof strength (Rp0.2). Draw a line parallel to the straight part of the curve, shifted 0.2% to the right, and read the stress where it crosses the curve.
Ultimate tensile strength (Rm)
The highest stress on the curve, just before the bar starts to neck.
Elongation (A) and reduction of area (Z)
How much stretch and thinning the bar survives: measures of ductility. Measure the broken bar, then calculate.

How to find Young's modulus (E)

E is the slope of the straight part of the curve: how much stress it takes to stretch the steel by a given amount.

  1. Click Zoom: start of curve so the straight part fills the graph.
  2. Move along the straight part and note the stress and strain at two points, well apart but both on the straight line.
  3. Subtract the lower point from the upper: E = (stress 2 − stress 1) ÷ (strain 2 − strain 1).
  4. The graph shows strain in %, so divide by 100 first (0.10 % = 0.0010).
  5. The answer is in MPa. Divide by 1000 for GPa.

Example: stress goes from 100 to 300 MPa while strain goes from 0.05 % to 0.15 %. Strain change = 0.10 % = 0.0010. E = 200 ÷ 0.0010 = 200,000 MPa = 200 GPa.
