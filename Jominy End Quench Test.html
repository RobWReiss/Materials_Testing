Jominy End Quench Simulator

Quench one end of a hot steel bar and see how deep the hardening reaches.

Why do we do this test?

To harden steel, you heat it red hot and cool it fast, which is called quenching. Fast cooling makes the steel hard. Slow cooling leaves it softer.

In a long product mill, the same grade of steel is rolled into bars of many different diameters. When a bar is quenched, the outside cools fast, but the middle of a thick bar cools slowly. A thin bar can harden all the way through, while a thick bar of the very same steel may stay soft in the middle.

The Jominy test measures this in a standard way. One end of a hot test bar is sprayed with water, so that end cools very fast and the far end cools slowly. One bar therefore shows every cooling speed at once. Hardness along the bar tells us how deep the hardening reaches, which tells us what size of bar the steel can harden through.

Carbon
decides how hard the steel can get, at the quenched end.
Alloying elements
such as chromium, molybdenum and nickel decide how far the hardness reaches along the bar.
